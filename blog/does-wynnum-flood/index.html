Does Wynnum flood?
What the council maps actually show.

The honest answer is: some of it, from some kinds of water, and council has mapped exactly which lots. This is how to read the maps for your own address, what the four kinds of flooding mean on the bay, and what it changes if you build.

Nathan Brain, carpenter and builder at Sabre 17 September 2026 5 min read

The short answer

Parts of Wynnum are mapped for flooding, and the source is not the Brisbane River. Wynnum sits on Moreton Bay, so the water council models comes from storm tide off the bay, from the creeks that drain to it, and from overland flow — rain running across land before it reaches a drain.

Brisbane City Council publishes the answer for every lot. The Flood Awareness Map shows the likelihood of each type of flooding for an address, and the FloodWise Property Report gives the levels: flood heights at different likelihoods, and the minimum habitable floor level a new house must meet.

Most Wynnum lots aren't affected by all four types, and many aren't affected by any. The ones that are usually still get built on — higher. Read the map for your address before you believe anyone, including us.

Which kinds of flooding does Wynnum actually get?

Council models four sources, and Wynnum's geography sorts them quickly.

  • River flooding. Wynnum isn't on the Brisbane River. This is the one people picture from 2011 and 2022, and it's mostly somebody else's problem here.
  • Storm tide. The bay, pushed up by a storm on a high tide. Mapped under council's coastal hazard overlay. This is the one that matters closest to the esplanade.
  • Creek and waterway flooding. Wynnum Creek and the drains to the bay back up when the tide is high and the rain is heavy at the same time.
  • Overland flow. Rain moving across land in a downpour. It follows old gullies and low points that the street grid was laid over, and it turns up on blocks nowhere near a creek.

How do you read council's map for your address?

  1. Open council's Flood Awareness Map and choose I am a resident, business or visitor.
  2. Search your address. The Flooding Overview panel lists the flood types and their likelihood for your property.
  3. Click Technical FloodWise Property Report for the levels.

“The Flood Awareness Map is an easy-to-use online tool that offers general flood awareness information. You can search for a property to see the likelihood of it being affected by different types of flooding.”

Brisbane City Council, FloodWise Property Reports. Council's answer for your lot beats anyone's opinion about your suburb, including a neighbour who's “never seen it flood.”

What does the FloodWise report tell you?

Three things you can act on.

  • Flood levels at different likelihoods — the 0.2%, 1%, 2%, 5% and 20% AEP events for river, creek and storm tide. A 1% AEP flood has a one-in-a-hundred chance in any given year. It isn't “once a century.”
  • The minimum habitable floor level for a new house on the lot.
  • Which City Plan overlays apply — river, creek, overland flow, coastal hazard — which decides whether there are flood planning requirements for a build.

“It is important to note that flooding may still present a risk and could include overland flow, which is shown as a property development flag further down the report.”

Brisbane City Council, FloodWise Property Reports, on lots with no river, creek or storm tide level assigned. Council also notes the report's aerial map does not show overland flow. Read the whole report, not the picture.

What don't the maps tell you?

  • Whether your house has ever been wet. The maps are models of likelihood, not a record. A dry history is good news; it isn't the same as no rules.
  • What a king tide does to the street. Nuisance tidal water on low esplanade streets is a different thing from a modelled storm tide, and the map isn't a tide chart.
  • What your insurer thinks. Insurers use their own flood data. Ask them, and tell them if a new house is designed to the current levels.

What does it change for a house?

If your lot carries a flood overlay, a new house has to meet the minimum habitable floor level in the report. Near the bay that often means the living areas go up: a highset house, or a ground floor of garage, laundry and storage with the family upstairs.

Done from the start, that's a better house, not just a compliant one. The rooms that matter sit where the water can't reach; the ground floor is built to be hosed out. Done as an afterthought — a plan drawn for a flat estate lot, then lifted — it's expensive and awkward. What an overlay does to a design is here.

It's also why the old houses matter. A 1960s lowset on a ground-level slab near the esplanade sits below levels that a new house wouldn't be allowed to. Sometimes the flood map is the strongest argument for starting again.

Key takeaways

  • Wynnum's flood risk is storm tide, creeks and overland flow — not the river.
  • Council has mapped every lot. Flood Awareness Map for the likelihood, FloodWise report for the levels.
  • 1% AEP is one in a hundred each year, not once a century.
  • The aerial map doesn't show overland flow. Read the whole report.
  • An overlay sets the floor level of a new house. Designed to it, that's a better house.

Common questions

Did Wynnum flood in 2011 and 2022?

We're not going to characterise past events street by street from memory. Council's flood information site includes historic flood mapping; read it for your address rather than taking anyone's recollection.

Is the esplanade the only part affected?

No. Storm tide is concentrated near the bay, but creek and overland flow mapping follows the low ground wherever it runs. That's why the check is by address, not by distance from the water.

Can we still build if we're in an overlay?

Usually. The overlay sets requirements — mainly floor height — that the design has to meet. It's paperwork and design, not a bar.

Can you read the report for us?

Yes. Bring it to the walk-through or we'll pull it together, and Nathan will tell you what the levels do to a design on your block.
