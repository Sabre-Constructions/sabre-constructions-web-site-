Your questions answered · Living on the Bayside

Moving to Brisbane's Bayside from interstate:
what nobody tells you.

You've looked at the map, seen the water, done the sums on the house prices back home and booked the removalist. Good. Here's the part the listings leave out: what the weather does, what the tide does, what the houses are made of, and the handful of local words you'll need by the end of the first week. From a family that builds here, not one selling here.

Nathan Brain, carpenter and builder at Sabre 17 September 2026 6 min read

The short answer

Seven things. The humidity is real from November to March and the houses here are built for it, which is why the old ones are up on stumps with the windows open. The tide goes out a long way on Moreton Bay, so “waterfront” means mudflats twice a day and the water some way off. Storm season runs October to March by council's reckoning, and the afternoon storm is a habit, not an event.

The train to the city is the Cleveland line, about forty minutes from Wynnum Central on the timetable we read. School catchments are drawn by the state and the house you buy decides the school. “Highset” is the word for a house lifted off the ground, and most of them are. And the houses themselves are mostly 1940s to 1970s timber and fibro, often with asbestos somewhere, on blocks that are bigger than the house deserves.

That last fact is the one that matters most for the next ten years, and it's the reason this post is on a builder's website.

What does the weather actually do?

Summer is hot and wet, not just hot. The humidity arrives around November and stays until the end of March, and the difference from a Melbourne or Adelaide summer is that the nights don't cool off much. Houses here cope with it by design: raised floors for air underneath, wide eaves, verandahs, windows on opposite walls for the breeze off the bay in the afternoon. The old ones were doing this before anyone had air-conditioning.

Then there's the storm season.

“Storm season typically lasts from October to March.” Council's advice covers roofs, gutters, trees and an emergency kit, and the afternoon storm with hail and wind is part of a Brisbane summer, not a rare one.

Brisbane City Council, Brisbane Severe Weather Alert. For what it means for an old house's roof and trees, this post.

Winter, by contrast, is the reason people stay: dry, bright, mild, and the best months on the water.

What's the tide about?

Moreton Bay is shallow and the tidal range is large enough that the water goes out a long way from the Wynnum and Manly foreshore at low tide, leaving sand and mudflats. Twice a day. It surprises people who expected a beach. What you get instead is the wading pool at Wynnum, the boat harbour at Manly, the jetty, and a foreshore that changes character every six hours.

The tide also matters for houses. Council maps storm tide as one of the kinds of flooding a block can be exposed to, and the streets one back from the water carry a minimum floor level for anything new. What the flood maps show is here.

How do you get to the city?

The Cleveland line. Wynnum has three stations (Wynnum North, Wynnum, Wynnum Central), then Manly and Lota. On the midday timetable we sampled, Central to Wynnum Central was forty minutes, and Manly and Lota a few minutes more. Peak services vary; check Translink for yours. By road it depends entirely on the hour. The honest version is here.

How do schools work here?

State schools have catchments drawn by the Department of Education, and a child is guaranteed a place at the school whose catchment the home address is in. The department's EdMap tool shows the catchment for any address. Independent and Catholic schools run their own enrolment. The practical point for a buyer from interstate: the house you buy decides the state school, so check the address before you fall for the house. The Bayside's schools and how catchments work are here.

The words you'll need

  • Highset / lowset. Lifted off the ground on stumps or posts, or sitting on it. More here.
  • Built-in-under. A highset whose underneath was closed in for rooms, usually decades ago.
  • Queenslander. The pre-war timber house: verandahs, iron roof, VJ walls, stumps.
  • Fibro. Fibre-cement sheeting. Pre-1990, it usually contains asbestos.
  • VJs. Vertical-joint timber wall boards. People pay to keep them.
  • The overlay. Council's character overlay; pre-1947 houses in it may not be allowed to come down.
  • Knockdown rebuild. What a lot of the streets are doing, one house at a time.

What are the houses like to live in?

Charming, and small, and tired, in roughly that order.

Seven in ten homes in Wynnum and Manly are separate houses on their own block, and most were built between the 1940s and the 1970s. That means timber frames that have lasted, rooms sized for a smaller family, kitchens and bathrooms from a different era, stumps that may have moved, and asbestos in the fibro somewhere. The blocks under them are generous. The houses on top often aren't.

Which is why so many families who move here for the street end up rebuilding on it a few years later. That story is here, and what the houses are made of is here. If you're buying the house for the block, check these eight things first.

Key takeaways

  • Humid summers and a storm season from October to March. The houses are built for it; the old ones were first.
  • The tide goes out a long way. Waterfront means mudflats twice a day and a foreshore that changes every six hours.
  • The train is the Cleveland line, about forty minutes to Wynnum Central on the service we read.
  • The address decides the state school. Check EdMap before the house.
  • The houses are 1940s–70s timber and fibro on good blocks. Charming, small, tired — and often rebuilt.

Common questions

Is it cooler by the water?

The afternoon sea breeze is real and the old houses were sited for it. It doesn't fix the humidity; it makes it bearable. A house designed to catch it matters more here than almost anywhere in Brisbane.

Do we need to worry about flooding?

Check council's free FloodWise report on any address before you buy. The Bayside's flood kinds are storm tide, creek and overland flow rather than the river. It usually changes what you'd build, not whether you'd live there.

Which suburb?

Wynnum is the town, Manly the harbour, Lota the quiet one, Manly West the flatter newer one, Wakerley and Gumdale newer again. The comparison is here.

Should we buy the old house and rebuild later?

Plenty do. Buy the block for the street and the school; treat the house as the thing you'll decide about once you've lived in it a winter. Nathan will walk it with you before you make an offer.
