What does Master Builders membership
actually mean for you?

Every second builder's ute has the Master Builders logo on it, and most families don't know what it means. It isn't a licence, and it isn't a guarantee. It is an association with a code of conduct, and a member has agreed to some things. Here's what it does and doesn't tell you, and how to check it in a minute.

Nathan Brain, carpenter and builder at Sabre 17 September 2026 5 min read

The short answer

Master Builders Queensland is the state's building industry association: a membership body with more than ten thousand members and offices across the state. A member is a business that has joined, pays to belong, and has agreed to the association's Code of Conduct, which requires members to conduct their business dealings in a fair, reasonable and honest manner.

What it is not: it is not a licence (the QBCC issues those), it is not a regulator (the QBCC is), and it is not insurance (the Queensland Home Warranty Scheme is, and it comes with a licensed builder whether or not they're a member).

So membership tells you a builder has chosen to belong to the industry's body and be bound by its code. That's worth something. The licence check tells you whether they're allowed to build your house at all. Do that one first.

What is Master Builders Queensland?

An industry association. It represents builders and trade contractors to government, runs training and licensing courses, publishes standard contracts, and provides its members with advice on contracts, disputes, workplace law and the QBCC's financial requirements.

It's a Queensland body with a long history and a large membership. Joining is voluntary. Plenty of good builders belong; some good builders don't.

What has a member agreed to?

The Code of Conduct. Its core obligation is the one that matters to a family:

Master Builders members agree to “conduct their business dealings in a fair, reasonable and honest manner” and to conduct themselves in a way that does not harm the association's reputation or that of other members.

Master Builders Queensland, Code of Conduct. An association's code is a promise to the association; the law's protections come from the QBCC Act. Both exist. They aren't the same thing.

Members also have access to the association's standard building contracts, which are among the common forms used for Queensland homes, and to its advice on getting the paperwork right. That tends to show up as a tidier contract on your kitchen table.

What membership is not

  • Not a licence. The QBCC licenses builders under the Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991. A member without a current licence in the right class cannot lawfully build your house. How to check the licence is here.
  • Not a regulator. If the work is defective or the builder walks off, your complaint goes to the QBCC, not the association. The association can hear a concern about a member's conduct; it can't order rectification.
  • Not insurance. The Queensland Home Warranty Scheme covers residential work over $3,300 done by a licensed contractor, member or not. The premium is collected by the builder and paid to the QBCC.
  • Not a quality mark. Membership says the builder joined and agreed to the code. It doesn't inspect their work.

How do you check a builder's membership?

Ask for the membership number and check it with Master Builders Queensland: the association has a member search on its website and answers the phone. Then do the check that matters more: put the QBCC licence number into the QBCC's online licence search and read the class, the status and the history.

A builder who puts both numbers in plain sight, on the site sign, the contract and the website, has made your job easy. Ours are in the footer of every page.

How much should it weigh in your choice?

As a tie-breaker and a signal, not a decider.

The things that should decide it are the licence and its history, the contract and its allowances, the deposit and payment terms, the dates, and the person you'll be dealing with when the frame is up. Those fifteen questions are here. Membership sits behind all of them: a builder who has chosen to be bound by an industry code and to use the industry's contracts is, on the whole, a builder who takes the paperwork seriously. That's a reasonable thing to like.

Key takeaways

  • Master Builders Queensland is an association, not a licence, a regulator or an insurer.
  • A member has agreed to a code: fair, reasonable and honest dealings.
  • The QBCC licence is the check that decides whether they can build your house. Do it first.
  • Home Warranty cover comes with any licensed builder, member or not.
  • Membership is a signal and a tie-breaker. Weigh it after the licence, the contract, the money, the dates and the person.

Common questions

Is a Master Builders contract better than a QBCC or HIA one?

They're all standard forms that have to comply with Schedule 1B of the QBCC Act. The differences are in the general conditions. Read whichever one you're handed with the QBCC's Consumer Building Guide beside it, and ask about anything that isn't clear.

Can we complain to Master Builders about a member?

You can raise a conduct concern with the association. For defective or incomplete work, the QBCC is the body with the power to act, and the Consumer Building Guide sets out its dispute process.

Should we avoid a builder who isn't a member?

Not on that alone. Check the licence, read the contract, ask the questions. Membership is one signal among several.
