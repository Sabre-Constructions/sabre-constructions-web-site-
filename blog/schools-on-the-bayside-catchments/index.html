Schools on the Bayside: the catchments,
and what “in catchment” is worth.

Catchment is the quiet reason behind half the rebuilds on the Bayside. The kids are in the school, the school is tied to the address, and the address is tied to a house that's run out of room. Here's how catchments actually work, where the schools are, and what that does to the decision.

Nathan Brain, carpenter and builder at Sabre 17 September 2026 6 min read

The short answer

In Queensland, every state school has a catchment: the area where its core intake of students must live. If your home is inside it, your child is entitled to a place. Outside it, you're applying, and a school with an enrolment management plan can say no.

You check yours on the Department of Education's EdMap by typing in your address. It's the address that counts, not the suburb name — a street can sit in one catchment and the next street in another.

On the Bayside the state schools are Wynnum State School, Manly State School, Lota State School, Manly West, Wondall Heights and Wynnum West for primary, and Wynnum State High and Brisbane Bayside State College for secondary, with Iona College and the Moreton Bay colleges as the independent options.

The catchment is why families stay in a house they've outgrown. It ties the school to the block. That's the decision this page is really about.

What is a school catchment in Queensland?

“A school catchment area is the geographical location where a state school's core intake of students must live.” “Catchment areas ensure every Queensland student from Prep to Year 12 is able to enrol at their local state school.”

Queensland Government, School catchment areas, qld.gov.au. The entitlement is to your local school. Anywhere else is an application.

Some schools also have an enrolment management plan. In the government's words, a plan ensures “enough places are kept for local students (i.e. those living in the school's catchment area)” and that “a fair process is in place for assessing enrolments for students living outside a school's catchment area.”

In plain words: a popular school fills up with local kids first, and out-of-catchment families join a queue that may never move.

How do you check which catchment you're in?

  1. Go to the Department of Education's EdMap site.
  2. Type your home address, or a school's name.
  3. The map shows the catchment boundaries and whether your address is inside.

Two things people get wrong:

  • The suburb name means nothing. Catchments follow streets and creeks, not suburb boundaries. Two houses in Manly West can be in different primary catchments.
  • Catchments change. Schools open, close and amalgamate. Wynnum State School itself is the 2010 amalgamation of three older primaries. Check the current map before you buy or plan around a school.

What is “in catchment” actually worth?

To a family, it's worth whatever not moving the kids is worth, and that's usually a lot. To the market, it shows up in what buyers will pay for a street, and we'll leave the number to the agents.

But here's the part the school guides don't say. A catchment is worth nothing if the house inside it doesn't fit your family. And it's worth exactly as much if you replace the house and keep the address.

Why catchment is a house question, not a school question

This is the conversation we have more than any other.

A family bought a post-war cottage in the right catchment when the kids were small. The kids are now at the school, settled, with friends. The house has two bedrooms and one bathroom. Moving means leaving the catchment, or paying for a bigger house in it, if one comes up.

The third option is the one the suburb keeps choosing: keep the address, change the house. On a flat Bayside block, a new home designed for the family — and for the flood level, and the aspect — keeps the catchment, the neighbours and the walk to school, and replaces the only thing that was wrong. It's one of the four reasons the Bayside is rebuilding.

Sometimes the honest answer is a raise or an extension instead. Sometimes it's a renovation. What it almost never is, once the kids are in the school, is a move.

Key takeaways

  • A catchment is where a state school's core intake must live. Inside it, your child is entitled to a place. Outside, you're applying.
  • Check your address on EdMap, not your suburb name. Boundaries follow streets, and they change.
  • Only Wynnum has a state high school; Manly and Lota feed into it. Wakerley has no state school inside the suburb.
  • Catchment ties the school to the block. That's why families stay in houses they've outgrown.
  • Keep the address, change the house is the option most families haven't priced.

Common questions

If we rebuild, do we lose our place at the school?

Your entitlement is tied to where you live. If you rent nearby during the build, ask the school how they treat a temporary address before you sign a lease — and rent inside the catchment if you can. We're builders, not the Department; check with the school.

Can we enrol out of catchment?

You can apply. A school with an enrolment management plan keeps places for in-catchment students first and assesses out-of-catchment applications by its own process. Ask the school directly.

Does a suburb's name tell me its schools?

No. Manly has a primary but no high school; Wakerley has no state school at all; Wynnum State School serves streets well beyond Wynnum. Use the map.

Are the independent schools in catchments?

No. Catchments are a state-school rule. Iona and the Moreton Bay colleges enrol on their own criteria.
