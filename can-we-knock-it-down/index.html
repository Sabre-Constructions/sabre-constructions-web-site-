Old houses and the rules

Old house?
Here's what the rules say.

If your house was built before the war, Brisbane has rules about what can happen to it. Here they are in plain words, and how to check your own house in about five minutes.

The short version

If your house was built after 1946, Brisbane's character rules almost certainly don't stop you knocking it down. Other approvals still apply, but the house itself isn't protected.

If it was built in 1946 or earlier and your street is in Brisbane City Council's Traditional building character overlay, demolition needs a development application, and council decides. It isn't automatically no. It isn't automatically yes either.

If it was built before 1911, council's position is that the house is to be kept.

What the overlay is

In council's own words, the overlay applies to “some areas and streets of older suburbs that have a distinct identity based on building character, materials, layouts and setbacks.” Its purpose is “to maintain the traditional, pre-1947 streetscape.” It's mapped street by street, so one side of a road can be in it and the other not. Wynnum, Manly and the older parts of the Bayside have plenty of pre-1947 houses, which is why it's the first thing we check.

When a pre-1947 house can still come down

Council's demolition code sets out the limited situations where it will say yes. Broadly, they cover a house that has been altered so much it no longer looks pre-1947, a house an engineer certifies is structurally unsound and can't reasonably be made sound, and cases where taking it down won't cost the street its character. Each of those has to be shown, not stated. We don't decide it, and neither does anyone else selling you a house.

How to check your own block

  1. Open council's City Plan mapping and type in your address.
  2. In the Map Layers menu, switch on “Traditional building character”.
  3. Download the Property Lot Report. It lists every overlay on your lot.

Or ring council on 07 3403 8888. Or answer two questions below and we'll do it with you.

Do we have to move out?

For a full knockdown rebuild, yes. There's no house to live in while the new one goes up. Most Bayside families rent nearby so the kids stay in the same school, and they plan the build around the school year. It's the biggest cost people forget, so it's one of the first things Nathan will talk through with you on the walk-through, before anyone signs anything.

Raises, extensions and renovations are different. Some of those can be worked around a family staying put, and Nathan will tell you honestly which yours is.

Source: Brisbane City Council, Heritage and character properties, and Brisbane City Plan 2014. This page is a plain-English guide, not planning advice. The mapping changes from time to time. Check the current map before you decide anything.
